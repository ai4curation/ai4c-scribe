# PR Description: Revise intraepithelial lymphocyte and add intestinal subclass

## Overview

This PR addresses issue #3346 by broadening the definition of "intraepithelial lymphocyte" (CL_0002496) to accurately reflect that these cells are found in multiple epithelial tissues, and by creating a new specific subclass for intestinal intraepithelial lymphocytes.

## Background

The original issue identified that the definition of intraepithelial lymphocytes was overly restrictive, limiting these cells to the intestinal epithelium only. In reality, intraepithelial lymphocytes are found in epithelial tissues throughout the body, including:
- Gastrointestinal tract
- Respiratory tract
- Reproductive tract
- Oral cavity

## Changes Made

### 1. Updated CL_0002496 (intraepithelial lymphocyte)

**Textual Definition Update:**
- **Previous**: "A T cell that is located in the intestinal epithelium and is capable of a mucosal immune response."
- **New**: "A tissue-resident lymphocyte located within the epithelial layer of mucosal tissues, particularly within the gastrointestinal, respiratory, and reproductive tracts, and oral cavity. Characterised by permanent residency, this cell typically expresses CD103 (integrin alpha-E), which binds to E-cadherin on epithelial cells, enabling epithelial retention in both mice and humans. Phenotypically, IELs display an activated, antigen-experienced state characterised by the constitutive expression of cytotoxic molecules (granzyme B, perforin) and innate-like receptors (such as NKG2D), enabling rapid, localised surveillance and immediate response to epithelial stress."

**Logical Definition Update:**
- **Previous**: `mature T cell AND (located_in some intestinal epithelium) AND (capable_of some mucosal immune response)`
- **New**: `mature T cell AND (located_in some epithelium) AND (capable_of some mucosal immune response)`
  - Changed UBERON_0001277 (intestinal epithelium) to UBERON_0000483 (epithelium)

**References Added:**
- PMID:29674648

A new specific subclass was created to maintain the original intestinal-specific definition while allowing the parent term to be more general.
